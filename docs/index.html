DjinnItAgreementForm

Fills in the UCS Court Reporter Minute Agreement Form and the MOFR for you, makes invoices from your transcripts, and keeps track of what you billed and what was paid. Drop in what the attorney sent, check the details, and get finished PDFs.

Download for Windows View on GitHub

Free and open source (AGPL). Windows 10 or 11. No administrator rights needed.

What it's for

Every private minute order starts with the same form: court, part, judge, case name, index number, dates, type of proceeding, rate, attorney and your own details. All of that is already sitting in the transcript, the invoice, the calendar photo or the e-mail. This app reads it and types it in, so you only check it.

How to use it

  1. Drop it inA PDF (transcript, invoice), a photo, or paste the e-mail text or a screenshot with Ctrl+V.
  2. Check the detailsEvery field shows where it came from. Guesses are highlighted, and a list offers other candidates.
  3. Pick speed and attorneyRates and the estimated delivery date follow your rate sheet. Buttons fill today, tomorrow, 1 to 3 weeks or 1 month.
  4. GenerateTick what to make (minute agreement, MOFR, invoice). The PDFs are saved and opened, one per attorney you tick. Ctrl+Enter does it from the keyboard.

A hundred orders? Drop the whole folder.

Batch mode reads any number of documents and sorts them into one job per case and date. A transcript and its invoice make one form, not two. Two days of the same case make two forms (or one, if you prefer). Tick the jobs you want and press Generate all.

The result

The court's own fillable form, filled in, with "per email" on the attorney line if you like, and your signature picture on the court reporter line. A clean re-typeset version and the original scan are available too. Sample shown uses made-up names.

Invoices from your transcripts

Drop a transcript PDF and tick Invoice. The page count and your rate sheet price the original, each party's copy and e-mailed copy, and an index for long transcripts, split between the ordering parties. By default the invoice lists Regular, Expedited and Daily so the attorney can choose, with your turnaround times and how to pay you. Each invoice gets its own number. Sample shown uses made-up names.

See what you billed, and what was paid

Records keeps every invoice and form the app made. See totals billed, paid and outstanding for any month, year or firm, with breakdowns by firm and by month. Tick Paid when the money comes in. Export a report, an Excel workbook or CSV files whenever you like; a CSV copy is always kept up to date in your Documents folder.

Good to know

Private

Everything runs on your computer. Photos are read with Windows' built-in text recognition. Nothing is uploaded.

Asks when unsure

Missing or conflicting items (index number, dates, judge) are raised before filling, never silently guessed.

Remembers you

Your name, address, phone, e-mail, default speed, copies and signature are set once in Settings.

Your rate sheets

Rates live in small spreadsheets (CSV) you can edit in Excel: private, city, whatever you charge.

Optional local AI

If you use Ollama, an AI helper can read messy photos and e-mails. It is off by default and stays on your machine.

Open source

Read the code, report a problem, or suggest a change on GitHub.

What you need

Windows

Windows 10 (version 1809, October 2018, or newer) or Windows 11, 64-bit. Not available for Mac or Linux.

Nothing else to install

Python and everything else the app needs are included. No administrator rights are required.

Space

About 200 MB of disk space. No internet connection is needed to use it.

For photos and scans

Reading photos uses Windows' own text recognition, which needs the English language pack. Most PCs already have it. Typed PDFs and e-mails don't need it.

Optional

The AI helper needs the free Ollama app and a small model, plus a reasonably modern PC. The app works fine without it.

Download and install

  1. Go to the Releases page and download DjinnItAgreementForm-Setup-x.y.z.exe.
  2. Double-click it and follow the usual installer steps. It installs just for you, with a Start menu entry and an optional desktop icon.
  3. Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the installer isn't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  4. On first launch, enter your name, address, phone and e-mail. Then drop in a document.
